# 2.1 Architecture and overview

Figure 1 organizes MOSAIC around the building-in-context record, a resolvable linked-data object for an individual structure. Source datasets remain in their existing repositories and formats, including StEER observations in DesignSafe, inventories and BIM, imagery, and hazard, site, and recovery data. MOSAIC describes and links these holdings in place, retaining their identifiers and access conditions while making their meaning and relationships machine-actionable.

Aim 1 (Section 2.2) produces the maintained specification governing this semantic interoperability layer. A natural hazards FAIR Implementation Profile maps StEER constructs to the Cross-Domain Interoperability Framework (CDIF), aligning existing standards for discovery, connection, meaning, structure, access, and interpretation. Versioned mappings and conformance criteria give producers and consumers a shared account of identifiers, units, time, geography, and permissible use.

Aim 2 (Section 2.3) produces records conforming to that specification. Each record binds structure attributes to experienced hazard intensity, site and exposure, functional role and criticality, and observations across pre-event, post-event, and recovery periods. Typed provenance distinguishes expert-assessed, machine-inferred, modeled, and validated assertions, with confidence and links to validating evidence. Explicit coverage distinguishes a field that is not applicable, applicable but not observed, or observed to be absent, preserving the conditions required for valid comparisons.

Aim 3 (Section 2.4) makes these records scientifically usable through reusable skills, query templates, transformations, validity checks, and shared memory of findings, failures, and corrections. Existing agents apply this expertise to regional and portfolio assessment and functional recovery workflows; MOSAIC does not require new foundation models or bespoke agents. Comparative evaluation on held-out events tests scientific correctness, traceability, reuse, transfer, expert effort, and cost against present-day portal and API access. Evaluation evidence feeds proposed corrections to Aim 1’s maintained specification, which in turn governs subsequent records and expertise artifacts. The three aims therefore form a learning loop: specification, conforming records, reusable expertise, and evidence-based revision.
